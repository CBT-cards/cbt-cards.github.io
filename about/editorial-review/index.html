Editorial review policy

How public CBT Cards educational resources are checked for sources, wording, safety boundaries, and freshness.

What is covered

The review registry currently covers all published resources classified in the public catalog as learning, worksheet, or toolkit-card. The machine-readable status is available at data/content-review.json.

What the review checks

  • Source links and factual wording still support the page.
  • General CBT information remains distinct from CBT Cards-specific product behavior.
  • Safety wording continues to preserve the general-wellness and self-reflection boundary.
  • Worksheet privacy statements still match the static no-submit browser behavior.
  • Curated toolkit cards remain consistent with their source record ID and publication-review status.
  • Product, privacy, or data-handling claims are not silently inferred from generic therapy concepts.

Review cadence

CBT Cards uses an internal target of reviewing covered resources at least once every 365 days. This is a project maintenance rule, not a clinical standard. The registry records last_reviewed and next_review_due for each covered resource.

The repository quality gate rejects a deployment when a covered resource is overdue. A meaningful source, safety, or product change should trigger an earlier review rather than waiting for the scheduled date.

What “reviewed” does not mean

Editorial review does not mean clinical validation, proof that an exercise is effective for a diagnosis, medical-device review, or a recommendation that an exercise is suitable for a particular person. CBT Cards does not make those claims.

Relationship to toolkit publication status

The toolkit publication overlay answers a different question: whether a raw source-corpus record has been reviewed for a standalone CBT Cards web page. The content review registry then tracks freshness for the curated page after it has been published.

Machine-readable status

Agents and tooling should use the stable catalog resource_id to join a page with its review entry. The registry intentionally tracks public resources, not individual users or app data.
