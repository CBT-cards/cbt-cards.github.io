CBT Cards / About

Useful ideas should outlive the app that carried them.

CBT Cards started as a mobile reflection app. The project now publishes a smaller, inspectable public layer that people can use directly and AI assistants can consume without guessing what is reviewed, current, or safe to reuse.

From product to public resource

The original mobile app combined visual cards, guided tools, journaling, and personal check-ins. The public project keeps the useful reflection material available beyond one app install and separates it from private app data.

That shift changes the quality bar. A public resource needs stable IDs, canonical URLs, evidence notes, safety boundaries, language status, review dates, machine-readable contracts, and tests that catch the project when its own claims drift.

What CBT Cards publishes now

  • Eleven CBT Cards-owned reviewed practices designed around plain-language situations, explicit mechanisms, small actions, and no_match safety behavior.
  • Learning guides and browser-local worksheets with narrow educational scope.
  • A pinned 115-record source toolkit with separate publication status and a record-complete editorial audit.
  • Evidence/provenance, semantic recommendation evals, stable RAG chunks, language metadata, schemas, and a versioned Agent Skills interface.

Three trust layers

Publication status says which raw source records CBT Cards has explicitly approved for a standalone canonical page. Unlisted source records stay source-only.

Source-corpus audit triages all 115 source records for overlap, framework attribution, overclaim, treatment-like wording, and future review priority. Candidate status is not publication approval.

Editorial freshness tracks when already reviewed public resources and owned practices were last checked and when they are due again.

Editorial principle

The project prefers a small reviewed layer over a large pile of near-duplicates. It is acceptable for the raw source corpus to contain material that remains unpublished. It is not acceptable to let the existence of a record quietly become a claim that CBT Cards recommends it.

Editorial review is not clinical validation. Evidence for a mechanism is not automatically evidence for the exact wording of a short card. Metaphors help memory; they do not prove claims.

Languages

English is the canonical source locale for the current curated knowledge layer. Russian currently has machine-draft overlays, and German is planned. Machine drafts are development data, not official localized guidance until separate human review and publication status say otherwise.

Original mobile app

The app remains separately documented through features, how it works, privacy, and support. Public-site releases, dataset changes, or agent-skill versions do not imply a new Android or iOS release.

Publisher

CBT Cards is published by MetalHatsCats. Public project data is read-only and does not expose a user's journal, check-ins, backups, or account.
