CBT Cards / About CBT Cards

Useful ideas should outlive the app that carried them.

CBT Cards began as a mobile reflection app. The project is now opening its practical material as a public library that people can use directly and AI assistants can use without guessing what the source meant.

Where it started

CBT Cards was originally built as a mobile app with short CBT-inspired reflection cards, guided tools, a structured journal, and personal check-ins for mood, stress, and energy. The visual cards and plain, practical language were the part people responded to most.

The app still exists, but an app install is no longer the boundary of the project. Useful prompts, worksheets, explanations, and source material can be more valuable when they are linkable, inspectable, reusable, and available outside one product interface.

Where it is going

CBT Cards is becoming a public reflection library for two kinds of readers: people and the assistants they choose to use. A person should be able to open a card or worksheet and understand it without technical background. An assistant should be able to find the same resource through a stable ID, canonical URL, structured data, and explicit language, safety, and review metadata.

The goal is not to wrap ordinary text in AI terminology. The goal is to make practical material easy to discover, cite, translate, test, and use responsibly across different interfaces.

What the project publishes

  • Reflection cards and toolkit resources that can be opened directly on the web.
  • Browser-local worksheets that do not submit what a person types to CBT Cards.
  • Plain-language learning guides with source links and visible review dates.
  • Machine-readable catalogs and datasets with stable resource IDs and canonical URLs.
  • Language and translation metadata that separates source material, draft translations, reviewed translations, and published localized pages.
  • Agent-facing instructions and data contracts for assistants, developers, and research tooling.
  • A public changelog that records website, data, translation, schema, and agent-interface changes separately from mobile releases.

Languages without pretending drafts are finished

English is currently the source language for the curated public knowledge dataset. Russian is being introduced as the first pilot locale, and German is planned. The project keeps language status explicit instead of treating every generated translation as publication-ready text.

Machine-generated translations can be useful development material, but they are not automatically official CBT Cards content. A localized resource needs separate language and editorial review before it can receive published status and a canonical public page. Translation records also keep the review date of the English source they were based on, so a later source update can make an older translation visibly stale rather than quietly inconsistent.

Editorial approach

  • Write for a person first. Plain language is not a temporary layer that gets removed when software arrives.
  • Keep a stable source record underneath different pages, translations, formats, and integrations.
  • Distinguish source material from records explicitly reviewed for standalone publication.
  • Distinguish machine translation drafts from reviewed and published localized content.
  • Use authoritative sources when a learning page explains a health-adjacent concept.
  • Keep provenance, review dates, safety limits, and uncertainty visible instead of filling gaps with confident prose.
  • Do not describe editorial review as clinical validation or evidence that a technique will work for an individual.

Health and safety scope

CBT Cards is for general wellness and self-reflection. It is not medical advice, diagnosis, treatment, emergency support, or a substitute for qualified professional care. Public worksheets are educational prompts rather than clinical assessments, and the project does not infer a condition from a person's answers, mood, stress, or energy check-ins.

The original mobile app

The mobile app remains available for people who prefer private on-device journaling, guided tools, cards, and check-ins in one place. Its product behavior, privacy details, and support information remain documented separately through app features, how it works, and the privacy policy.

Publisher and contact

CBT Cards is published by MetalHatsCats. For practical help, privacy questions, or current support options, use the support page. Do not send private journal contents or other sensitive personal information when reporting an issue.
