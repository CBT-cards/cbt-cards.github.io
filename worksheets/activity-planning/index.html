Activity planning worksheet

A practical worksheet for making one activity specific, reducing the first step, choosing a time and place, and reviewing what you notice afterwards.

Read the activity-planning guide →

1

Choose one activity

Name one task or activity, not an entire project or life area.

2

Why does it matter?

What would doing this support: a responsibility, connection, enjoyment, learning, care for yourself or someone else, or simply reducing unfinished business?

3

Make the first step smaller

What is the smallest visible action that would count as starting? Prefer something concrete enough that you can tell whether it happened.

4

Choose when and where

Decide when you will try the first step and where you expect to be. A specific cue is usually easier to act on than “sometime later.”

5

Plan for one obstacle

What is most likely to get in the way? Make the response modest and practical rather than trying to solve every possible problem in advance.

6

Review after trying it

What actually happened? What did you notice about effort, mood, energy, stress, enjoyment, or willingness to continue? These observations are personal notes, not clinical scores.

7

Choose the next step

Based on what happened, repeat, reduce, adjust, or choose the next small action.

Source and limits

This worksheet follows the small-step and activity-planning approach described in NHS Every Mind Matters self-help CBT resources. Read the NHS guide.

Activity scheduling is also used within behavioral activation, but this public worksheet is a general planning and self-reflection resource. It is not an individualized treatment plan and does not claim that completing an activity will change a particular symptom or condition.
