CBT thought record

A structured way to slow down a difficult moment and examine the thoughts, feelings, and information around it.

What is a CBT thought record?

A thought record is a common CBT exercise for writing down a situation, the thoughts and feelings connected with it, and information that may support or challenge an initial interpretation. The goal is not to force a positive thought. It is to slow the process down enough to examine what happened and consider a more balanced or useful perspective.

A common sequence

  1. Situation: describe what happened as concretely as you can.
  2. Feelings: note the emotions and, where useful, their intensity.
  3. Initial thoughts: capture the thoughts or images that showed up.
  4. Evidence: consider information that supports and does not support the initial thought.
  5. Alternative view: write a more realistic, neutral, or balanced interpretation.
  6. Review: notice whether your feelings or next action look different after the exercise.

How CBT Cards relates to a thought record

The CBT Cards journal uses a structured entry with fields for a situation, thoughts, emotions, body reactions, behaviour, alternative thoughts, outcome, and tags. It is a reflection format inside the app; CBT Cards does not interpret an entry, score it as clinically significant, or tell you whether a thought is correct.

When to keep it simple

A short entry is still an entry. If you are using a thought record for personal reflection, it can be more practical to describe one specific moment than to reconstruct an entire day. If an exercise is making distress worse or you need individualized help, stop using the exercise and seek support from an appropriate qualified professional.
