Activity planning

Turn “I should do something” into one small, observable action you can actually review afterwards.

Why plan activities?

When motivation is low or a task feels uncomfortable, avoiding it can provide short-term relief. A structured activity plan makes the next step smaller and more specific. It can also help you notice whether your week contains a workable balance of routine tasks, necessary tasks, and activities you find enjoyable or meaningful.

A practical sequence

  1. Name one activity: choose something specific rather than an entire project.
  2. Make it small: define a first step that can be started in a few minutes.
  3. Choose time and place: decide when and where you are more likely to begin.
  4. Plan for one obstacle: decide on a modest response to the most likely thing that could get in the way.
  5. Do the activity: focus on completion of the planned step rather than judging motivation beforehand.
  6. Review the effect: notice what changed in mood, energy, stress, enjoyment, or willingness to continue, if anything.
  7. Choose the next step: repeat, reduce, adjust, or move to the next small action based on what actually happened.

Use the free worksheet

The free activity-planning worksheet turns the sequence into seven browser-local fields. It can be printed or saved as PDF, has no submit action, and does not treat your observations as clinical scores.

In CBT Cards

CBT Cards includes activity-effect entries and timers alongside reflection cards and the journal. These can help you record what you did and how it felt before or after. They are personal observations, not clinical outcome measures.
