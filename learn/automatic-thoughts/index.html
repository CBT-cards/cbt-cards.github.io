Automatic thoughts in CBT

Fast thoughts are common. The useful skill is noticing the ones that matter in context, not treating every thought as a verdict.

What are automatic thoughts?

In CBT, automatic thoughts are the quick thoughts or images that can appear in response to a situation. They may arrive so quickly that you notice the emotion or urge to act before you notice the thought itself.

The useful question is not “How do I eliminate automatic thoughts?” Everyone has fast thoughts. Reflection becomes more concrete when you notice a thought that is connected with a strong feeling or important behaviour and examine it in context.

A simple noticing exercise

  • What was happening just before the feeling changed?
  • What went through my mind at that moment?
  • Was it an observation, an interpretation, a prediction, a memory, or an image?
  • What did I do next?

You do not have to decide immediately whether the thought is true or false. Capturing it accurately is often the first useful step.

In CBT Cards

You can use a reflection card or a structured journal entry to record the situation, thoughts, emotions, body reactions, and behaviour. CBT Cards does not label a thought as a symptom, distortion, or diagnosis.
